# PRD Quality Review — Vivah Spot

## Overall verdict

This is an unusually well-argued PRD: it has a real thesis (the whole wedding as the matching unit, trust built before engagement because no money is held), it names what it gave up rather than balancing everything to neutral, and §7 does genuine load-bearing work including explicit supersessions of the prior Scope document. The 67 FRs carry mostly falsifiable consequence bullets, which is where most PRDs fail. What is at risk is the quantitative floor: §5 declares itself incomplete, there is no security or authentication requirement anywhere, and every threshold a dev agent needs — review window length, grace period, statutory takedown periods, performance and availability targets — is absent, so an autonomous build will invent them. Two of four surfaces (Admin, guest RSVP) have no journey to design from, and the Glossary — declared binding on downstream artifacts — attaches six core objects to the "couple" that FR-2 explicitly says does not own them.

## Decision-readiness — strong

A decision-maker can act on this. Choices are stated as choices with the cost named: §1 concedes the no-money model is "a weaker instrument than holding the money, and it is stated as such"; FR-8's **Known limit** admits the platform "can never know what was **paid**"; UJ-3 records a rejected alternative by name ("letting the non-cancelling party review, which rewards stonewalling"); FR-29 accepts that "A Vendor who does not respond is not penalised." The cross-journey dependency between UJ-1's matching accuracy and UJ-2's calendar discipline is stated at the seam rather than buried, and §7.1 refuses the easy dodge — "This is not a deferral. It is the commercial model."

The gap is the opposite of the usual one. There are **zero** `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, **zero** `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts, and no Open Questions section in an 88KB document produced from a coaching session. That is defensible as a stance for an autonomous build, but it means genuinely inferred values — eight-year retention (FR-43), 6/12-month subscription terms (FR-52), the three-tier structure (FR-50) — are indistinguishable from confirmed ones, and a downstream agent has no way to know which are safe to question.

### Findings

- **medium** No open-items register anywhere (§ whole document) — 0 `[ASSUMPTION]`, 0 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, 0 Open Questions across 67 FRs. Values that must have been inferred (FR-43's "**eight years**", FR-52's "6 or 12 months", FR-50's three tiers) read as decided, so nothing is flagged for confirmation before an autonomous build acts on it. *Fix:* tag the inferred values inline with `[ASSUMPTION: …]` and add a short Assumptions Index; the count will be small, which is itself the useful signal.
- **medium** Externalized dependency with no landing place (§4.10, FR-50) — "Tier prices are a business input, set outside this document" is the only pointer, and FR-52 requires a Vendor to "select a term of 6 or 12 months and pay online". Nothing says where prices come from, who supplies them, or what a build does in their absence. The same applies to FR-50's tier differentiators — "portfolio allowance, and how many Services the Vendor may list in" — which are named but never valued. *Fix:* state that tier prices and allowances are configuration seeded by the client before launch, and say so in §7 or an assumptions entry rather than in a single subordinate clause.

## Substance over theater — adequate

Little of this is furniture. The Vision in §1 could not be swapped into another PRD — "a town like Shrirampur", "fifteen or more vendors by phone, from memory, on the strength of who someone's cousin used last year" is specific enough to be wrong, which is the test. Three personas, two of them named and carried consistently through every UJ and most FR prose; each drives real decisions (Rutuja forces FR-9's state-the-shape-once, Dattatray forces FR-3's one login and FR-23's Spaces). The differentiation claim in §4.7 — that a qualified Enquiry answers "the lead-quality complaint that drives vendors off other platforms" — is a mechanism, not a boast, and it costs nothing because "the Workspace already knows all of it."

The exception is §5, and it is a large one. §5.3 Performance and §5.4 Availability contain no numbers at all — "remain usable on low-bandwidth connections and low-end devices", "sized for peak muhurat load, not for average load", "Vendor-facing surfaces stay available during peak". These are precisely the boilerplate-shaped NFRs the rubric flags: they cannot be failed, so they cannot be met.

### Findings

- **critical** NFR theater in the two sections that most need bounds (§5.3, §5.4) — no latency target, no payload budget, no device or Android-version floor, no definition of "low-bandwidth", no uptime target, no peak concurrency figure, no SLO on the vendor surface whose unavailability the section itself says "costs a Vendor a wedding". Architecture and story creation source their sizing here and will find adjectives. *Fix:* give each a number and an instrument — e.g. a p75 device class, a search/Block-match response budget on 3G, an image weight ceiling per listing card, an expected peak-week concurrent-session figure, and an availability target for the vendor portal during muhurat weeks.
- **low** FR-67 inspiration boards are the thinnest link to the thesis (§4.14, FR-67) — the FR is defined mostly by what it does not do ("does not shortlist it, enquire about it, or affect the budget", "no following, no public board, and no interaction"). Unlike FR-65, which earns its place by turning Agreements into verified evidence, boards serve no stated part of the whole-wedding or trust argument. *Fix:* either connect boards to a decision the family actually makes, or move them to §7 so they do not consume build capacity on a no-phasing plan.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated, not implied, and the features follow from it. §4.3 opens "This is the capability that makes Vivah Spot worth opening" and the whole group is derived from the single insight that the unit is the wedding, not the vendor. §4.9 states the dependency chain explicitly — "Reviews are the only consequence a Vendor faces on Vivah Spot. There is no money to withhold and no delisting for poor service" — which is why the anti-gaming machinery in FR-44 through FR-49 is proportionate rather than paranoid. §4.11's framing of the Lead Dashboard as "the entire renewal argument" ties a screen to the revenue model in one line.

The Success Metrics genuinely validate the thesis rather than measuring activity, and every one carries a counter-measure. SM-7's counter-measure — "the proportion of Candidate Blocks that clear a family's whole Shortlist. **This is the honest test of whether matching is doing anything.**" — is the sharpest line in §6 and is exactly the metric a weaker PRD would omit.

Which makes the one hole conspicuous. SM-7 concedes that below a supply-density threshold "cross-Service Block matching produces no useful answer" and that "no other metric will reveal that" — and no FR in §4 addresses supply acquisition at all.

### Findings

- **high** The thesis feature has a stated precondition no requirement owns (§6 SM-7; §4.3; §4.10 FR-51) — cross-Service Block matching is worthless below a supply density the PRD names but never pursues. FR-51's Founding Vendor tier is a pricing construct, not an acquisition mechanism, and there is no requirement for vendor invitation, referral, bulk onboarding, or a coverage view telling Admin which Service in which place is under-supplied. Given §7.3 forbids the platform supplying its own listings, supply is the single-point failure of the whole product. *Fix:* add an FR for an Admin supply-coverage view keyed to FR-33's place hierarchy, and state whether vendor acquisition is a platform capability or an explicit §7 non-goal handled operationally.
- **medium** SM-1 measures a flow no FR describes (§6 SM-1; §4.1, §4.12) — "median time from a Vendor starting sign-up to their Listing being published" is the first success metric, but the path from FR-1's phone sign-in to FR-58's Verification gate to a published Listing has no requirement: what the Vendor supplies, in what order, how Verification is requested and scheduled, what they see while waiting, whether a draft Listing persists. *Fix:* add a vendor onboarding FR to §4.1 or §4.12 covering the draft-to-published path and the Verification handoff.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

The FR-level craft here is above average. Most consequence bullets are genuinely testable — FR-6's enumeration of what an invited member cannot do, FR-32's "shown **by name** every Shortlist entry and every Agreement", FR-43's cryptographic digest and trusted time source, FR-20's "position within them cannot be purchased — by any tier, add-on or arrangement". The rubric's usual targets ("handles gracefully", "user-friendly") are largely absent from §4.

But the document is systematically missing every number. §5 announces this about itself — "*Cross-cutting requirements. Written as they are settled; the section is incomplete.*" — while §0 sets the opposing standard: "Anywhere this document is silent or vague, an agent downstream will decide for itself, and that decision will be nobody's." Both cannot be true of a chain-top artifact for an autonomous build, and the PRD does not say which wins or list what §5 still lacks.

The specific holes matter because they sit under the mechanisms the PRD calls load-bearing. The review window's *closing* time is referenced four times (§2.2 UJ-3, FR-45, FR-48 twice) and never given a duration — yet FR-48's "published when the window closes even if the other side never wrote one" is the entire retaliation defence, and its behaviour is undefined without one. FR-53's "**grace period**" has no length. FR-63 defers three separate statutory clocks to "the periods required of an intermediary", "the required period" and "the shorter period that applies to such orders", and §5.5 to "the period required" — in a PRD that ships alongside a 107KB `research-india-regulatory.md`. A dev agent will either guess or re-run the research, and either way the number will be nobody's.

### Findings

- **critical** §5 declares itself incomplete without saying what is missing (§5 preamble) — "the section is incomplete" is the only signal, and it contradicts §0's decision-closure standard. Downstream architecture cannot tell whether an absent NFR is a deliberate silence or an unfinished one. *Fix:* either complete §5 or replace the preamble with an explicit list of the named gaps and who closes them, so silence stops being ambiguous.
- **critical** No security or authentication requirements anywhere (§5; §4.1 FR-1) — FR-1 is the entire auth specification and says only that a code is sent to a phone number. Nothing states OTP expiry or retry limits, sign-in rate limiting, session lifetime (FR-1's "not challenged again on every launch" is the closest thing and is unbounded), account-takeover handling on SIM reuse, or any protection for the Admin surface that FR-4 gives unrestricted access to every capability. This is a platform holding guest contact data (§5.5) and eight-year Agreement records (FR-43). *Fix:* add §5.x Security with auth bounds, session policy, Admin-surface protection, and storage/transit requirements for the two data classes §5.5 already distinguishes.
- **critical** Every threshold that gates a stated mechanism is unspecified (FR-45, FR-48, FR-53, FR-63, FR-29, §5.5) — review window duration; subscription grace-period length; grievance acknowledgement, disposal and takedown periods; breach reporting period; the nudge trigger (UJ-2's "you have N enquiries for the 27th" leaves N and cadence undefined); guest-data erasure timing ("erased once the purpose is exhausted"). Each is the operative value of the requirement it sits in. *Fix:* fill each from `research-india-regulatory.md` where it is a statutory clock, and decide the rest; these are single-value edits with disproportionate downstream effect.
- **high** `Commitment` is a Glossary term with no FR, and FR-45 depends on it (§3; §4.9 FR-45) — the Glossary defines a Commitment as "a Vendor-declared, publicly published promise: delivery timeline, inclusions, no hidden charges", and FR-45 opens the review window when "the Vendor's own committed delivery date passes". No FR requires a Vendor to declare a delivery timeline, states where it is published, or says what happens when a Service has none. The anti-retaliation fallback — "**A Vendor cannot hold it shut by delivering late**" — has no requirement behind its trigger. *Fix:* add an FR in §4.5 requiring a declared Commitment per Listing, whether it is a condition of listing (FR-59), and the window behaviour when no delivery date exists.
- **medium** The empty permitted set is unhandled (§4.5 FR-24, FR-25) — FR-24 limits the family to the permitted set where a restricting Rule is in force, FR-25 makes that set the Vendor's Preferred Vendors, and FR-25 also requires that "**A named Vendor must accept the association before it is published.**" A venue with a "no outside caterer" Rule and no accepted Preferred caterer leaves the family unable to engage catering at all. FR-32 covers the collision at engagement time but not this case. *Fix:* state whether a restricting Rule requires a non-empty accepted permitted set as a condition of listing, or what the family is shown and may do when the set is empty.

## Scope honesty — strong

§7 is the strongest section in the document and does real work. Nine subsections of explicit exclusions, each with reasoning rather than a list — §7.1 distinguishes commercial model from deferral, §7.3 explains why bundles would make the platform appear to stand behind supply, §7.6 draws the line at automating the family's judgement ("The platform shows the collision. The family resolves it."). Supersessions of the prior artifact are stated where they occur rather than left to be discovered: §5.2 "*This supersedes Scope §9 and §11, which deferred iOS*", §7.2 "*This supersedes Scope §2 objective 4, §5.3 and §8*". §7.9 bans vocabulary across "the PRD, the UI, the schema and every downstream document" and pre-empts the obvious contamination — "The existing prototype code contains 'Book Now', 'My Bookings' and a bank-details payout section… must not be treated as specification." §7's own framing — "An agent that finds a gap here should treat it as a gap, not as permission" — is exactly the right instruction for the build model.

Two things are omitted without being placed.

### Findings

- **high** FR-47 grants a dispute route that §7.2 excludes (§4.9 FR-47; §7.2) — FR-47 says of the Vendor's structured assessment that "The family can see everything recorded about them and can dispute an entry with Admin", while §7.2 excludes "any complaint pipeline that leads to a consequence" and "adjudication of fault". These may be different things — contesting a record about yourself versus complaining about service — but nothing says so, and a build agent implementing FR-47 must invent the dispute workflow, its outcomes, and whether Admin may alter or annotate the entry (which §5.7's append-only rule also touches). *Fix:* define the dispute route's mechanics and outcomes in FR-47, and add a sentence to §7.2 distinguishing it from the excluded complaint pipeline.
- **medium** Notifications are unowned, and the WhatsApp consent analysis is applied only to guests (§4.2; FR-29, FR-35, FR-53) — §4.2's "Why the family sends" reasons carefully that "WhatsApp requires that a recipient have both given the business their number and opted in before a business may message them", then FR-29, FR-35 and FR-53 have the platform message Vendors on WhatsApp with no opt-in requirement stated and no FR group owning notification delivery, retries, template approval, or fallback when WhatsApp is unavailable. Vendors are customers so consent is obtainable, but the PRD never says it is obtained. *Fix:* add a notification FR covering channel, consent capture at vendor onboarding, and fallback, or state in §7 that notification mechanics belong to architecture.

## Downstream usability — adequate

Structurally this is set up well for extraction. FR IDs run 1–67 with no gaps and no duplicates, every FR carries a `Realizes UJ-N` tag, and every internal cross-reference resolves (FR-22→FR-15, FR-31→FR-30, FR-32→FR-17, FR-27→group 4.12). Each feature group opens with a Description that carries enough context to be pulled out alone, and §0 tells downstream artifacts that the Glossary is binding.

The Glossary itself is excellent on the novel domain objects — Candidate Block, Chosen Block, Slot, Space, Delivery, Anchor Date are defined precisely enough to build a schema from, including the input/output distinction between Candidate and Chosen. It is silent on every actor and role noun the FRs actually turn on, and where it does name an actor it names the wrong one.

### Findings

- **high** The Glossary attaches six core objects to the "couple" that FR-2 says does not own them (§3; §4.1 FR-2) — the Glossary defines Wedding as "the parent object **a couple** creates", Workspace as "**the couple's** command centre", Shortlist as "the set of Listings **a couple** has saved", Enquiry as "**couple**-initiated contact", Contact Reveal as "the point at which **a couple** obtains", and Agreement as terms "confirmed by both **a couple** and a Vendor". §4.1 states the opposite — "The person planning a wedding is very often not the person getting married… The couple are names on the Wedding, not the owners of it" — and FR-2 makes couple names "stored as data on the Wedding, independent of the account that owns it". The FR body uses "family" 126 times against the Glossary's 13 "couple". A downstream agent building the data model from the binding Glossary will attach ownership to the wrong entity. *Fix:* rewrite those six definitions in terms of the owning account, and define "family" as the Glossary term the FRs already use.
- **high** No Glossary entry for any actor or role the FRs depend on (§3) — missing: *family*, *Wedding creator* / *owning account* (FR-2, FR-5, FR-6, FR-34, FR-39, FR-65 all turn on "only the Wedding's creator may…"), *invited member* (FR-5, FR-6, FR-22), *Admin* (FR-4, FR-14, FR-58, FR-60–64), *Lead Dashboard* (referenced inside the Contact Reveal definition and in FR-36, FR-51, FR-53, and given its own feature group, but never defined), and *profile* (FR-17, FR-42 record cancellations "on both profiles"; no FR defines a profile surface for either side). §0 makes the Glossary binding, so the terms it omits are the ones that will drift. *Fix:* add entries for these six; the permission model in §4.1 in particular cannot be extracted safely without a defined creator/member distinction.
- **medium** Orphan capability in the Glossary (§3, Workspace) — the Workspace definition lists "dashboard, budget tracker, guest list, **checklist**, Shortlists, Enquiry tracking". "Checklist" appears nowhere else in the document; §4.2 has no FR for it and FR-10's enumeration of the single view omits it. A build agent working from the binding Glossary will either invent a checklist with no requirements or silently drop a promised capability. *Fix:* add an FR for it in §4.2 or remove it from the definition.

## Shape fit — adequate

The shape is broadly right for what this is: a multi-stakeholder consumer marketplace feeding UX, architecture and stories, where journeys with named protagonists are load-bearing. UJ-1 through UJ-3 do that job unusually well — each has a named protagonist carrying context inline, an entry state, a numbered path, a climax that names the thing the product does that nothing else does, and an edge case with a stated resolution. The "Confirmed constraints carried by UJ-N" blocks after each journey are effectively a UX brief, and UJ-3's constraint list is where most of §4.8 and §4.9 comes from. The decision not to phase is correct for a chain-top artifact and is defended in §0 and enforced in §7.9.

The mismatch is coverage. §5.2 names four audiences across three surfaces, and §4.2 FR-12 adds a fourth unauthenticated one. Two of them have no journey at all.

### Findings

- **high** The Admin surface has eight FRs, its own web panel, and no UJ (§2.1; §5.2; §4.12, §4.13) — §2.1 gives Admin a full JTBD list ("Verify a vendor in person and record that it was done, by whom, on what basis", "Remove a fraud quickly, and leave the record of why"), §5.2 gives them a web panel, and FR-4 and FR-58 through FR-64 give them the platform's entire trust spine and its regulatory compliance. There is no journey showing an Admin doing any of it, and FR-4 and FR-61 dodge the tag with "Realizes the Verification and removal obligations behind UJ-2 and UJ-3" rather than naming a UJ. UX has nothing to design the console from, and FR-62's "define a new Service — its taxonomy, fields, filters, comparison attributes, sizing attribute, engagement model and pricing model — without a code release" is the most complex screen in the product with no journey behind it. *Fix:* add UJ-4 with a named Admin protagonist covering the verification visit through to a published Listing, and UJ-5 or an extension covering Service configuration.
- **high** The guest RSVP surface is the sole growth channel and has no protagonist (§4.2 FR-12; §6 SM-6) — FR-12 creates a hosted page for people who "record an RSVP without an account, an install or a sign-in", declares that the link preview "is the platform's principal surface in front of guests", and routes growth through "a quiet route for a guest planning a wedding of their own". SM-6 makes it "the only growth channel designed into the product". No journey shows a guest opening the link, and the conversion path a guest follows into their own Wedding is described only as "a quiet route". *Fix:* add a short UJ with a named guest protagonist covering invitation receipt through RSVP to the point of consent, since this is the one surface whose design determines SM-6.

## Mechanical notes

- **ID continuity:** FR-1 through FR-67 are contiguous, unique, and complete — no gaps, no duplicates. FR-32 and FR-33 appear out of numeric order inside §4.5 (sequence there runs 23, 24, 25, **32**, 26, **33**, 27), evidently later insertions. Harmless under §0's stable-global-ID policy, but a reader scanning numerically will lose them.
- **Cross-references:** all resolve. FR-22→FR-15, FR-31→FR-30, FR-32→FR-17, FR-27→"group 4.12", FR-25→FR-24, FR-13/FR-19 consistency on the "shows available" attribution, and the three external supersessions (Scope §9/§11, Scope §2 obj 4/§5.3/§8) are cited by section.
- **`Realizes` tag form:** FR-4 and FR-61 use prose instead of an ID — "Realizes the Verification and removal obligations behind UJ-2 and UJ-3" / "Realizes the obligations behind UJ-2 and UJ-3". Every other FR uses `Realizes UJ-N`. Machine extraction of the UJ→FR matrix will fail on exactly these two, which is a symptom of the missing Admin UJ.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** nothing to check — no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and no index. See the Decision-readiness finding.
- **UJ protagonists:** all three named and carried inline (Rutuja, Dattatray, both). No floating UJs. Persona context, entry state and edge case present in each.
- **SM count vs header:** §6 opens "*Five outcomes, stated by Abhishek*" and lists seven (SM-1..SM-7). SM-6 and SM-7 each carry an italic note explaining they are not among the five, so this is honest rather than sloppy — but the header still reads as a miscount on first pass.
- **Glossary drift:** "couple" vs "family" is the significant one and is raised under Downstream usability. Otherwise discipline is genuinely tight — Candidate Block, Chosen Block, Slot, Space, Enquiry, Agreement, Delivery and Verification are used verbatim throughout, and the two "Deliberately not used" entries (*Booking*, and *v1/MVP/Phase N*) hold across all 1,153 lines.
- **Required sections:** Vision, Target User, Glossary, Features, NFRs, Success Metrics and Scope Boundary all present. Missing for a chain-top autonomous build: an Assumptions Index, an Open Questions register, and a security NFR.
